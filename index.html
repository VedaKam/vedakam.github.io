Hi! I'm Veda.

ML Research & Engineering

Cornell University B.S. & M.Eng.

Bay Area, New York, etc.

I build machine learning systems for real-world data — spanning biological, clinical, and operational domains — with a focus on interpretability, reliability, and real-world deployment. My current thesis work uses a Borzoi-based cross-attention model to predict spatial gene co-expression across mouse embryo development, in the De Vlaminck Lab at Cornell.

My work aims to:

  • Build models that are evaluated reliable, not just accurate on a benchmark
  • Focus on mechanistic interpretability — understanding what's actually happening inside models, not just how well they perform
  • Think about the "last-mile" crisis in healthcare — the gap between a model performing well in a paper and being usable, trusted, and adopted by clinicians in practice

Questions I'm currently interested in:

  • What causes models to generalize on benchmarks but fail on the signal that actually matters?
  • How do we build AI systems domain experts can trust and audit, not just ones that perform well on paper?
  • Where does interpretability actually change deployment decisions?

Previously, my work at Harvard's Wyss Institute included co-authoring "Tissue Regenerative Medicine: Clinical Advances, Challenges, and Opportunities" (APL Bioengineering), alongside research on graph-based drug repurposing pipelines.

I've also worked as a Software Engineering Intern at Revinci.ai, building a cost-attribution system that tracks per-call inference cost against revenue to assess AI agent profitability, and led Cornell DEBUT's development of SteadyStride, a self-stabilizing cane for Parkinson's disease that won 1st place at the Medtronic Design Competition.

Outside of work, I create music and explore new coffee shops to log on Beli (@vedakam).
